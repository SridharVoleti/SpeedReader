# World 1 passage-length amendment v0.26

**Decision source:** `Requirements/SpeedReader_Decisions_Acceptance_Criteria_Codex.xlsx`, DEC-018 through DEC-021 and the `Passage Progression` worksheet. **Confirmed by product owner:** the new rules govern passage 151 onward.

This amendment supersedes only the passage-length transition statements in `SpeedReader_W1_BandA_Spec_v0.25.md` that say passage 151 begins a 200-word band (the summary near line 55 and the transition rule near line 1060), plus the same old band-transition wording in `Content Creation/SpeedReader_World1_Blocker4_BandA_Readiness_Spec_v1.0.md`. Those statements are retained as historical text. The first 150 passages remain exact-100 Band A content, with the same 15-strand competency architecture, assessment definitions, evidence sufficiency, and independent readiness gates. Passage 151 does not waive Band A readiness.

## New length schedule

- 1–150: 100 words.
- 151–175: 125 words; 176–200: 150; 201–225: 175; 226–300: 200.
- Repeat three 25-passage bridge steps, then a 75-passage consolidation at the next 100-word milestone, ending with 1426–1500 at 1,000 words.
- The target is generated by `passageLengthTarget` in `lib/world1-product.ts`; the workbook's `Passage Progression` sheet is the reference table.
- The reading span remains one word at a time at the Band A transition. No simultaneous deliberate speed increase occurs on a length-step event.
- A longer passage being served does not itself award a stamina level. Successful validation of each new 25-word capability step can award exactly one, subject to the configured confirmation count and independent comprehension/oral gates.

## Regression review

`tests/unit/world1-product.test.ts` checks every sequence through 1500, representative bridge and milestone boundaries, sequence integrity, no automatic stamina award, and speed hold at a length transition. Production readiness still requires approved catalog content, the canonical word counter, and the frozen KM readiness suite for passages 1–150.
